02|The Big Three

NVIDIA beats, guides higher, and keeps its checkbook half open

What it is: NVIDIA reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion on Tuesday, up 103 percent from a year earlier, with data center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110 percent, and said Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped. NVIDIA guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, above the roughly $125 billion analysts expected, and assumes no data center compute revenue from China. On the call, interim chief executive Colette Kress said NVIDIA will stay disciplined and selective in supporting neoclouds; asked whether that was new, she said, "Is it new? No. Did we change anything? No." Purchase commitments rose to $390 billion from $279 billion, mainly multiyear memory buys. Neocloud commitments were $32 billion, and total guarantees were $118 billion, including $105 billion of credit support for the PORTS-Pike campus that OpenAI will lease for 20 years. Before Wednesday's open, NVIDIA rose about 3.5 percent, CoreWeave 5.4 percent, and Nebius 7 percent (Reuters).

Why it matters: Monday's fear was that NVIDIA would pull its financing; Tuesday showed a narrower tap, not a shutoff, with demand still ahead of supply. The bigger signal is memory: the largest chip buyer is locking up years of supply, which pushes prices up for everyone else.

NVIDIA's board says no to Huang and starts a search with Haas in the frame

What it is: Jensen Huang said Tuesday morning that he would be honored to return as chief executive if the board decides that is in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company, and America. Hours later, board chair Harvey Jones said on the earnings call that directors had voted unanimously to accept Huang's resignation and do not intend to reinstate him, and that NVIDIA has hired Heidrick & Struggles to run a thorough search of insiders and outsiders. The Wall Street Journal reported that Heidrick has begun talks with candidates including Arm chief executive Rene Haas (via Quartz and Investing.com). Semafor reported that a board faction still wants Huang back over the objections of three directors, that Haas and Marvell's Matt Murphy have been considered, and that Haas worries about the optics of replacing Huang. Jeff Bezos urged NVIDIA to hire from inside. Arm shares rose 4.3 percent in Tuesday morning trading after a Morgan Stanley forecast lift (Investing.com).

Why it matters: The board has chosen a process, not a person, and a split board slows any outside hire. The outcome decides who sets NVIDIA's terms on chips, financing, and export compliance next year, and Arm's own chief executive is in the frame.

OpenAI ships its first public model rated High for speeding up AI research

What it is: OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra, its frontier model for professional work, and GPT-6.2 Sol, its everyday model, on Tuesday. OpenAI says Astra is its first externally released model to meet the High threshold for AI self-improvement, which it equates to giving every OpenAI researcher an extra, highly capable research engineer compared with 2024. Both models meet OpenAI's Critical threshold for cybersecurity and launch with tighter safeguards for risky security requests. Astra's API prices are 20 percent lower than GPT-6 Astra's, at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens; Sol stays at $2 and $10. Artificial Analysis ranks Astra second on its Intelligence Index at 62, behind Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 at 65, and Epoch AI ties the two on its capabilities index (via The Decoder).

Why it matters: A lab that California sued on Monday shipped a model on Tuesday that it says speeds up its own research, with Control Room monitoring and stronger containment as the guardrails. Labs keep shipping, and the checks now travel with the release rather than ahead of it.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

Scotiabank cuts first-pass credit analysis from an hour to under a minute

Who and what: Scotiabank launched Commercial Navigator on November 17 for its entire Canadian Commercial Banking team: a Financial Statement Analysis Agent that drafts key ratios, trends, and client questions on new deals, and a Credit Administration Agent that digitizes credit document review. Both run on Scotia Intelligence, the bank's in-house data and AI platform. Model, chips, and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Initial financial analysis on new deals fell from one hour to less than one minute, and credit document review is up to 30 percent faster. The bank aims to give each employee back the equivalent of one month of administrative work a year by the end of 2027. Tag: Likely (company-reported, no baseline published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: a narrow, measurable task inside an existing credit workflow, rolled out to a whole business line. Weakness: the one-minute figure covers only the first pass, people still make the credit decisions, and the one-month goal is a target, not a result.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

Apollo Hospitals closes most safety reviews within three days with an agent

Who and what: Apollo Hospitals in India uses the Daily Tiered Huddle agent, built with Microsoft's forward-deployed engineers on Microsoft Foundry and Fabric, to pull patient data, summarize adverse events, and suggest case assessments for its clinical governance team, which reviews about 600 incidents a month across 55 main hospitals, per a Microsoft feature on November 17. The agent is in production at some hospitals. Model and chips: not disclosed.

Measured result: Over 60 percent of cases reviewed by the huddle now close within three days (November 2026 compared with December 2025), and patient falls and medication errors fell in July to September 2026 against the same quarter of 2025, with no counts published. Staff satisfaction with how errors are handled rose almost 30 percent in Apollo's survey. Tag: Likely (customer and vendor-reported).

Strength and weakness: Strength: faster triage frees clinicians for the 40 percent of cases that need a deeper look, and the agent flagged a cluster of central line infections in one unit. Weakness: the safety gains carry no published counts or controls, and cluster analysis is still a work in progress.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

5. Microsoft makes Arm-based Cobalt 200 servers generally available.

At Microsoft Ignite on Tuesday, Microsoft made Azure Cobalt 200 virtual machines generally available. Built on Arm Neoverse Compute Subsystems (CSS) V3, they deliver up to 50 percent better processor performance than Cobalt 100, per Microsoft, and Microsoft plans for Cobalt 200 to be a significant portion of new Azure compute capacity in 2027 and beyond, per Arm. Named users include Amadeus, Databricks, InMobi, OpenAI, SAP, and Snowflake.

+Bull

Agents run code, call tools, and query databases on general-purpose processors, so a faster in-house processor lowers Azure's cost for agent work and leaves more power for accelerators.

−Bear

The 50 percent figure is Microsoft's own comparison with its previous chip, and Arm's forecast that Arm-based servers will pass 60 percent of server share at leading hyperscalers by the end of 2026 is a vendor projection.

My call (Certain on general availability (Arm Newsroom); Likely on the performance and share claims (company-reported))
Every top cloud now sells its own Arm-based processor at scale (Guessing on Microsoft's mix), and x86 share of new hyperscale capacity keeps falling in 2027.
Who wins
Microsoft's margins on general compute; the named users.
Who loses
x86 server vendors in Azure's new capacity.

06|Unknown knowns
Public facts the market is underweighting. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

NVIDIA's biggest guarantee backs one OpenAI campus, not the neoclouds

Headlines read "disciplined and selective" as a neocloud story, but the numbers point elsewhere: of NVIDIA's $118 billion in total guarantees, $105 billion is credit support for land, power, and shell at the PORTS-Pike campus, which will host only NVIDIA hardware under a 20-year lease to OpenAI. Neocloud commitments, which take effect only if partners secure financing and NVIDIA accepts the deployment, fell to $32 billion. NVIDIA's credit exposure is now concentrated in one lab's site.

Confidence: Certain on the figures (NVIDIA earnings call); Likely on the prior quarter (Yahoo Finance); Guessing on the concentration effect

Memory is the line item that more than doubles next year

NVIDIA's purchase commitments rose $111 billion in one quarter, mainly for multiyear memory. On Wednesday in Seoul, TrendForce forecast the average price of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) to rise 122 percent in 2027, from $1.67 to $3.70 per gigabit, with HBM4 at $3 to $5.50, and said it does not expect conventional dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) prices to fall next year. TrendForce also sees smartphone production down 7.8 percent and laptop shipments down 10.6 percent in 2027 as memory shifts to servers.

Confidence: Certain on NVIDIA's commitments (NVIDIA); Likely on the forecasts (TrendForce via Chosunbiz)

Open-weight models went from 7 to 31 percent of tracked enterprise tokens in four months

Databricks' new AI Index, published Monday, says open-weight models grew from 7.3 percent of enterprise token traffic through its Unity Gateway in July to 30.9 percent in November, and the share of those enterprises using a single model provider fell from about 64 percent to 28 percent. DeepSeek V4.1 and GLM 5.3 tie for third on its coding benchmark at a 93.1 percent pass rate. So far, cheaper open models add inference buyers rather than shrinking demand.

Confidence: Likely (Databricks, company data on its own customers); Guessing on the demand effect

07|Unknown unknowns
Surprises from outside the big players. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

Cerebras teams AMD chips with its wafer-scale systems for five times the tokens per megawatt

Cerebras put disaggregated inference into production on Tuesday: AMD Instinct graphics processing units (GPUs) process the prompt, and Cerebras CS-3 systems generate the answer. On a shared GPT-OSS-120B endpoint measured November 14, the pairing delivered five times the throughput per megawatt of CS-3 systems alone, with no loss in generation speed. Amazon Web Services, Fireworks AI, Gimlet Labs, and General Compute have early access, and Cerebras plans hundreds of megawatts of these clusters by the end of 2027. When power is the limit, mixing chip types to raise tokens per megawatt becomes the product.

Confidence: Likely (Cerebras, self-measured on one endpoint); Guessing on the industry effect

An open-source attack framework plugs into the labs' own models

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) warned in a FLASH advisory, released Friday and amplified widely on Tuesday, that criminals and state-aligned hackers use ARTEX, an open-source agent framework, to automate intrusions from reconnaissance to data theft, with some reaching privileged access in under three hours. ARTEX carries no model of its own and calls outside models, including Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok, and Kimi. The FBI ties it to a ConnectWise backup compromise with attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 downstream tenants. Containment inside a lab does not cover models reached through public APIs.

Confidence: Certain on the advisory (FBI); Guessing on the effect on lab safeguards
