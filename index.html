02|The Big Three

NVIDIA's board opens a probe into Jensen Huang

What it is: On Sunday, November 8, NVIDIA's board said a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will investigate reports that founder and chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, and the company said Huang will keep leading without interruption. The New York Times and Financial Times reported that United States intelligence assessed Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, as working for China's Ministry of State Security; Megaspeed is under a Commerce Department probe over chip smuggling. In a staff email Huang apologized for being unfaithful to his wife and said he will cooperate fully. Three Republican House committee chairs asked him to a closed-door briefing on November 18. Shares fell about 5 to 6 percent before Monday's open, to about $175.49.

Why it matters: The company that supplies most AI accelerators now carries leadership and export-policy risk at once. Analysts flagged the chance of much tighter China chip rules, which would reprice who may buy and rent NVIDIA systems: the permission half of Part 3 of the thesis.

Anthropic keeps its IPO date and accepts a lower price

What it is: The Financial Times reported Anthropic will press ahead with an initial public offering before Thanksgiving, raising $80 billion to $100 billion at a valuation of $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion, up from its current $965 billion but short of the $2 trillion some investors hoped for a month ago. One large investor expects pricing near $1.5 trillion; others expect about $1.3 trillion. Anthropic holds about $150 billion of cash and liquid investments, and JPMorgan plans an information session for prospective investors on November 13. The Information reported the roadshow goes ahead this week. Separately, Bloomberg reported OpenAI completed an $11 billion portion of its funding round at $1.25 trillion, with the round possibly reaching $20 billion.

Why it matters: Capital still flows to frontier labs, but at prices set by a market that just sold AI infrastructure hard. Tripwire 1 on Anthropic's IPO moves from a timing risk to a price test, and lower lab marks flow into every compute contract underwritten on lab growth.

Microsoft prunes its neocloud roster before CoreWeave reports

What it is: CNBC reported on November 6 that Microsoft is shrinking its roster of neocloud partners to focus on a few key suppliers while building its own data centers; it has not decided which to drop. Named partners include CoreWeave, Nebius, Chronoscale, IREN, Lambda, Nscale, and Oracle. Bloomberg reported Microsoft is winding down a 2023 deal to rent AI capacity from Oracle; Data Center Dynamics says the contract covered older NVIDIA hardware for Bing and newer contracts appear unaffected. IREN said a Microsoft networking upgrade request moves its capacity online to January or February. Microsoft said its strategy is "built on flexibility and choice." CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN shares are down roughly half over the past month. CoreWeave reports Tuesday after the close; Microsoft was 62 percent of its 2024 revenue.

Why it matters: The largest renter of neocloud capacity is shifting from renting to owning. Demand is not shrinking, but optional expansions and renewals at neoclouds are now at risk, and Tuesday's CoreWeave print is the first test of whether contracted backlog holds.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

Bombardier's AI sales engineer cuts four-hour range maps to seconds

Who and what: Bombardier built Clara, an agentic sales engineer on Salesforce Agentforce, for business-jet sellers. Clara answers specification questions from more than 500 technical documents and runs range calculations through Aircraft Performance Group's interface and Bombardier's wind models, shown on Azure Maps. Salesforce published the story on November 6. Model and chips: not disclosed.

Measured result: A range-map calculation that took at least four hours of engineering work now takes 10 to 20 seconds; 97 percent accuracy on complex technical calculations; 5 out of 5 user satisfaction at launch. Tag: Likely (Salesforce customer story; test population not disclosed).

Strength and weakness: Strength: sellers answer buyers live instead of waiting up to four days across time zones. Weakness: the accuracy test set and error types are not published, and the satisfaction score has no stated sample size.

Cost and time to deploy: Built and deployed in 15 weeks with Salesforce Professional Services and forward-deployed engineers; dollar cost not disclosed. Now scaling to more than 300 sellers.

Maturity: Production.

BMO puts an AI agent beside card-dispute staff to cut call time up to 30 percent

Who and what: BMO launched AI Agent Assist on November 6 in its Virtual Connect contact center for employees handling credit card transaction calls. The agent matches transactions, retrieves policy, and prepares cases; staff review every decision. Model, chips, and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: No measured result yet. Baseline: more than 500,000 calls a year at 12 to 14 minutes each. Targets: 20 to 30 percent less time on eligible calls and about 40 percent of eligible refund requests processed automatically. Tag: Certain on the baseline; Guessing on outcomes (targets only).

Strength and weakness: Strength: a high-volume, measurable workflow with a human in the loop and reusable parts across the bank. Weakness: only targets so far, with no error or refund-accuracy figures.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed; part of BMO's goal of more than $1 billion of AI-enabled value by 2030.

Maturity: Production.

Anthropic's dreaming agents lift answers on a 420 million token archive

Who and what: Anthropic ran Claude Opus 5 agents with dreaming, an offline pass that reviews transcripts to fix notes and build a linked wiki, over about 350,000 internal documents (roughly 420 million tokens) from Stainless, a software company it acquired in May. Published November 6. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Scores on 25 graded questions rose from 13 percent to 48 percent as dreaming scaled from 2 to 128 sessions; with search plus the wiki, 61 percent versus 53 percent with search alone, saving about 20,000 tokens per question. On 24 merger questions, 87 percent with the wiki versus 73 percent with search. Tag: Likely (company evaluation, no outside audit).

Strength and weakness: Strength: self-correcting memory raises accuracy on work that spans many documents. Weakness: dreaming costs compute, and on single-document tasks the wiki showed no clear gain or grew larger than its sources.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed; about 20,000 tokens saved per question when wiki and search are combined.

Maturity: Pilot.

4. Anthropic buys 1.7 gigawatts of its own gas generators.

The Financial Times reported Anthropic agreed in late September to pay about $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas generators by taking over OnePWR Solutions' Caterpillar orders, placed about two years ago before price increases. OnePWR will help install them, and delivery is expected within two years. The FT called it the largest known single purchase of power equipment by an AI lab. Sites were not disclosed; Reuters could not verify, and neither company commented.

+Bull

Owning generation shortens time to power and hedges multi-year grid connection queues.

−Bear

On-site gas brings permitting and emissions fights, the same permission risk Texas just raised.

My call (Likely (FT report relayed by CNBC and DCD; not confirmed by Anthropic))
Power is now a lab procurement line item. Part 3 of the thesis gets its clearest proof yet.
Who wins
Caterpillar, OnePWR, gas suppliers, and on-site power builders.
Who loses
Grid-dependent projects waiting in connection queues.

5. AMD EPYC hosts Google's first confidential TPU system.

AMD said Monday that 5th Gen EPYC processors power the host infrastructure for Google Cloud TPU 8tx, the first cloud Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) system with hardware-enforced confidential computing. AMD Secure Encrypted Virtualization keeps prompts, model weights, and intermediate data encrypted in host memory and in transit to the accelerators. AMD says EPYC is the host processor for more than 10 leading hyperscalers and frontier labs.

+Bull

Confidential computing on accelerators opens regulated workloads such as health and finance to cloud TPUs.

−Bear

No pricing, availability dates, or performance figures were published.

My call (Certain (AMD release with a Google Cloud quote))
Host processor choice is now a security decision, not only a cost one.
Who wins
AMD; Google Cloud customers with sensitive data.
Who loses
Host processor rivals without an equivalent attested link to accelerators (Guessing).

METR measured AI speeding up Anthropic's own research by about 1.9 times

METR, an outside AI evaluator, spent about 11 weeks inside Anthropic and reported on November 5 that benchmark progress since October 2025 ran about 1.9 times faster than before (95 percent interval 1.5 to 2.8), mostly from AI-assisted research, with no clear near-term bottleneck. Markets read it as a safety story; it is also a compute demand story. Anthropic redacted much of the evidence.

Confidence: Certain on METR's published figures; Guessing on the demand read

Microsoft's Oracle exit covers 2023 hardware, not the newer contracts

Data Center Dynamics, citing Bloomberg, says the wound-down deal ran older-generation NVIDIA chips for Bing and that newer Microsoft and Oracle contracts appear unaffected. Oracle says customers still pay a 20 percent premium for four-year-old hardware. Headlines read as a broad Oracle snub; the facts point to a generation refresh.
