02|The Big Three

Arm's record quarter proves the data center story

What it is: Arm reported second-quarter fiscal year ending 2027 total revenue of $1.443 billion, up 27 percent, against guidance of $1.38 billion plus or minus $50 million. License revenue rose 50 percent to $773 million; royalty revenue rose 8 percent to $670 million with data center royalties doubling. Arm raised confidence in up to $2 billion of Arm AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027 and 2028, named Oracle Cloud Infrastructure for 2027 instances, and said Arm-based server share at top hyperscalers rose from 15 percent in 2024 to more than 50 percent in 2025.

Why it matters: Arm printed the data center and AGI CPU narrative in hard numbers: licensing leading royalties, hyperscaler share above half, Oracle named. Soft phone royalties and a muted Q3 royalty guide are why the stock did not celebrate the beat.

OpenAI pays its own power bill; capacity in hand is the moat

What it is: OpenAI published a National Community Compact committing, for campuses it owns and operates, to pay the full cost of electricity and supporting grid infrastructure. CNBC and Ballotpedia counted 41 local data center ballot measures on November 3 across 10 states, with voters backing restrictions or rejecting pro-development measures in every contest with results available except Harwood, North Dakota. CoreWeave bonds tumbled; Microsoft will not more than double its Nscale Texas deal.

Why it matters: Yesterday established permission as a fourth bottleneck. Today's news is the industry response and the market response: a frontier lab absorbing grid costs in writing, credit punishing debt-funded builders, and a hyperscaler declining optional neocloud capacity.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

OpenAI's agent pipeline validates and patches 948 Linux LPEs in one run

Who and what: OpenAI security researchers ran an autonomous cyber vulnerability discovery, exploitation, and patching pipeline against the Linux kernel, using an upcoming cyber model, with human review before maintainer reports. Mid-October 2026 research run; public write-up November 4, 2026.

Measured result: Dynamically reproduced 1,724 vulnerabilities; 948 distinct working local privilege escalation (LPE) exploits plus 291 information-leak proofs of concept; total API cost $1,213,430 ($1,279 per LPE); at least 10 novel exploitation techniques claimed. Tag: Certain (OpenAI primary blog).

Strength and weakness: Strength: prices defender labor and ships candidate patches at scale. Weakness: dual-use offensive capability is held back; human review comes after exploit generation; company-reported techniques cannot be independently replayed without the model.

Cost and time to deploy: $1,213,430 API credits for the main run; about $32,936 for the kernelCTF-style campaign that found 21 LPEs.

Maturity: Pilot.

Anthropic's biology team maps the first prokaryotic vault particle with Claude

Who and what: Anthropic's biology research lab used Claude across sequence search, DNA construct design, isolation method optimization, and cryo-electron microscopy processing to characterize vault particles from bacteriophage and bacterial proteins. Published November 4, 2026. Advisor: Leonard Rome (vault discoverer).

Measured result: First 3D structure of a vault from prokaryotic life (L05 phage vault): 96 protein copies, about 54 by 45 nanometers, about 25 percent more enclosed volume than mammalian vaults despite being shorter. Conventional sugar-gradient purification takes about three days; Claude-aided size-exclusion method took about 90 minutes from cells to microscopy grid. First full structural model ready in 34 hours (team estimates weeks manually). Seven of 15 expressed proteins formed vault-like particles; four confirmed by cryo-electron microscopy. Tag: Likely (company research post; measured lab times are self-reported).

Strength and weakness: Strength: shows agent-assisted science compressing weeks of structural work into days, and opens a simpler bacterial system to study a 40-year eukaryotic mystery. Weakness: biological function of vaults still unknown; Claude also caused a two-week setback with a bad growth-medium additive and an early flawed mirror map; function claims would be Guessing.

Cost and time to deploy: Compute for cryo-electron microscopy used hundreds of GPUs in parallel; dollar cost not disclosed. Most other steps said to be doable with existing Claude products.

Maturity: Pilot.

Together Compiler agents re-optimize inference for production traffic

Who and what: Together AI launched Together Compiler (TKCC) on November 4, 2026: agents that continually rewrite and validate GPU inference code against live traffic shapes, models, and hardware, hot-reloading candidates in a persistent engine. Serving production traffic today per company.

Measured result: Up to 2.6 times higher tokens per second than leading InferenceX open-engine configurations across frontier open models (Kimi K3, GLM 5.3, MiniMax M3.1-Flash, DeepSeek v4.1-Flash) in under 24 hours; 1.7 times versus open megakernels on Llama 1B batch-1; state-of-the-art low-latency claims of 1,111 tokens per second on MiniMax M3.1-Flash and 1,205 on DeepSeek v4.1-Flash; 252 times higher experiment throughput versus prior agent optimization systems. Company X post also claimed "up to 50 percent faster on customer workloads." Tag: Likely (company benchmarks; no independent audit found).

Strength and weakness: Strength: AI improving the systems that run AI, adapting as traffic drifts. Weakness: all speedups are vendor-reported; customer 50 percent claim is thinner than the InferenceX 2.6 times claim.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed. Optimization loop uses 2 nodes per compile in reported InferenceX tests; production serves "trillions of tokens" per day.

Maturity: Production.

Defenders can make finding and fixing bugs cheaper than historical bug bounties ($20,000 to $70,000 per LPE at Pwn2Own and TyphoonPWN implies 16 times to 55 times the pipeline cost). Automated patching becomes necessary as models find bugs faster than humans.

The same capability is dual-use; "human in the loop" is a review step after autonomous exploit generation; OpenAI is not releasing the cyber model, which limits outside verification.

My call (Certain on OpenAI's published numbers and method)
This is the clearest measured agent deployment of the window: labs keep shipping while containing a capable offensive model rather than releasing it.

Linux maintainers receiving patches; defenders who adopt similar pipelines; sandbox vendors (Firecracker, gVisor) that OpenAI flags as blast-radius controls.
Who loses
Software projects that are not rescanned as models improve; operators who treat an unpatched low-privileged account as safe.
