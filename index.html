02|The Big Three

NVIDIA's founder is out, and its finance chief is in charge

What it is: NVIDIA's board announced Thursday that Jensen Huang resigned as president, chief executive, and director, effective immediately, after a Wednesday night board meeting. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, at NVIDIA since 2013, is interim chief executive and stays finance chief; Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive, becomes the company's first board chair, and the board will consider internal and external candidates. The exit followed Huang's admitted affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials believe worked for Chinese intelligence, and a Wednesday report of a second affair with an employee who reported to him. Shares fell about 10 percent before the open, and third-quarter results remain set for Tuesday, November 17, at about 1:20pm Pacific Time.

Why it matters: NVIDIA supplies, invests in, and backstops much of the AI buildout, and its board says customer commitments and the roadmap continue without interruption. The bigger change is political: Huang was the industry's most effective voice against tighter export rules, according to Eurasia Group's David Meale. AMD rose more than 3 percent and Broadcom edged up, while NVIDIA-backed CoreWeave fell 14 percent and Nebius 20 percent premarket (Yahoo Finance).

Neoclouds swap bonds for customer cash and secured loans

What it is: Nebius reported third-quarter revenue of $980.8 million on Thursday, up 571 percent and above the $907.5 million consensus, but now expects 2026 revenue at the low end of $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion because some third-party capacity slipped into the first half of 2027; the contracts behind it remain in place. Capital spending rises to about $27 billion this year and $40 billion to $45 billion in 2027, against 2027 revenue guidance of $12 billion to $13 billion. Customer prepayments covered more than 100 percent of capital spending on deals signed in the quarter, up from 50 to 60 percent in the second quarter. Separately, Bloomberg reported Wednesday that CoreWeave plans at least $4 billion of investment-grade loans, led by Morgan Stanley and MUFG and backed by chips and contracts, with early pricing talk of 7 to 7.5 percent, about half its junk-bond yield.

Why it matters: The two largest public neoclouds have found cheaper money than junk bonds: their customers, and loans secured by contracts. Nebius expects prepayments and contract-backed financing to cover 75 to 85 percent of its 2027 spending. The limit moved to delivery: the delays sit at colocation sites, including Microsoft capacity in New Jersey, where the site operator is switching from temporary generators to Bloom Energy fuel cells. Nebius shares fell 16 percent Thursday (CNBC).

Washington moves to close the overseas chip rental loophole

What it is: The Commerce Department is finalizing a long-awaited rule to stop Chinese AI companies from remotely using advanced chips in data centers abroad, The Information reported Wednesday, covering Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway, and South Africa, with end-user reporting duties for advanced-chip imports there. Axios reported that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent asked Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick in a Situation Room meeting why Megaspeed is not on the Entity List, the US trade blacklist; Commerce denies the exchange happened and has not added any company to the list in more than a year. Singapore barred Alice Huang from re-entering for giving police inaccurate information but found insufficient evidence of offences in its Megaspeed probe.

Why it matters: With Huang gone, chip-control bills in the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), including the Chip Security Act and the MATCH Act, are harder to strip out, analysts told CNBC. The rule lands days before President Trump meets Xi Jinping at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen on November 18 to 19, and Chinese firms are reportedly stockpiling NVIDIA capacity abroad ahead of tougher enforcement.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

EV charging help lines run on open Gemma models and Google TPUs

Who and what: lemonflow, a European startup, runs AI support agents that answer electric vehicle (EV) drivers stuck at chargers by phone, chat, email, and WhatsApp in more than 90 languages, reading live charger logs and restarting chargers remotely, per a company-written Google Cloud post on November 11. The agents self-host Google's open Gemma 4 model on Cloud TPU v6e chips in the EU, with fallbacks to self-hosted GPUs and Gemini Enterprise; background monitoring agents run on Gemini.

Measured result: Agents handle more than 80 percent of conversations end to end without a human handover and earn 95 percent driver satisfaction; median response latency fell to about 350 milliseconds, roughly three times faster than the managed service it replaced; prompt test cycles dropped from 2 to 3 hours to about 10 minutes; Gemma answers more than 99.9 percent of live turns. The service covers more than 40 charging networks in 17 countries and more than 250,000 charge points. Tag: Likely (company-reported on the vendor's blog; no satisfaction baseline published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: owning the model and chips removed rate-limit errors and kept latency flat at peak, and a rules layer screens prompts before the model sees them. Weakness: about one conversation in five still goes to a human, and every number is self-reported.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

Chinese banks move AI from pilots into daily operations

Who and what: Chinese banks are running AI on document handling, customer service, payment-fraud detection, and early credit assessment, Caixin reported on November 11. Bank of Jiangsu, Shanghai Pudong Development Bank, and Bank of China gave figures, and Moonshot's Kimi model is deployed at ICBC, CICC, Citic Securities, and E Fund Management. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Bank of Jiangsu says AI has replaced 1.2 million working hours cumulatively and cut approval time for small and midsize business credit by 30 percent; Shanghai Pudong Development Bank says its digital workforce handles work equal to more than 2,500 people a year; Bank of China's systems recognize 305 document types across 3,800 business scenarios. Tag: Likely (bank-reported figures via Caixin).

Strength and weakness: Strength: rules-based, high-volume tasks show clear time savings. Weakness: investment research still needs human judgment, fragmented data and weak coordination between business and technology teams slow integration, and private deployments take about three months to update versus about two days for hosted versions.

Cost and time to deploy: A single private AI project can cost several million to tens of millions of yuan, excluding maintenance (a Tencent WorkBuddy source, via Caixin). Time to deploy: not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

1. NVIDIA's founder is out, and its finance chief is in charge: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

The board says operations, customer commitments, and the product roadmap continue without interruption, Kress has run NVIDIA's finances since 2013, and results still land November 17 as scheduled.

−Bear

A founder-led company has no named successor, Bernstein halved its price target from $400 to $200, and NVIDIA has lost roughly $2 trillion of value since its October 6 peak.

My call (Certain on the transition (NVIDIA release) and the price target (CNBC); Likely on the value lost (Axios estimate))
Demand for NVIDIA's chips does not change this quarter, but its political shield does. Expect buyers to add second sources faster and Washington to tighten export rules with less pushback.
Who wins
AMD and Broadcom, which rose on the news; Google's tensor processing unit (TPU) business; China hawks in Congress.
Who loses
NVIDIA shareholders; NVIDIA-backed neoclouds such as CoreWeave and Nebius, which fell premarket; advocates of looser export controls.

2. Neoclouds swap bonds for customer cash and secured loans: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

Customers prepaying more than the cost of the build, plus secured loans at about half of junk-bond yields, mean the strongest neoclouds no longer depend on the high-yield market.

−Bear

Nebius raised 2026 capital spending to about $27 billion and guided $40 billion to $45 billion for 2027, far above the $28.8 billion analysts expected, and its delays came from sites it does not control.

My call (Certain on Nebius figures (Nebius release and shareholder letter; Reuters; CNBC); Likely on the CoreWeave loan (Bloomberg via Quartz; terms not final))
Financing stops being the binding limit for the two largest public neoclouds. Delivery, power hookups, and colocation partners take its place.
Who wins
Investment-grade lenders such as Morgan Stanley and MUFG; on-site power suppliers such as Bloom Energy; neoclouds that own their sites.
Who loses
Junk-bond investors losing deal flow; colocation partners that miss dates; labs tying up cash in prepayments.

3. Washington moves to close the overseas chip rental loophole: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

Clear rules written now are easier for operators than months of uncertainty, and the Trump and Xi summit next week is still expected to extend the trade truce.

−Bear

Data centers in Southeast Asia, Brazil, Norway, and South Africa face new reporting duties, Chinese buyers are reportedly stockpiling ahead of the rule, and Beijing could answer new chip laws with rare earth curbs.

My call (Likely (The Information via Investing.com and Chosun Biz; Axios, whose account Commerce denies; CNBC))
Remote access is the next export control. Clouds outside the US with Chinese tenants carry the compliance cost, and NVIDIA no longer has its most effective lobbyist to slow it.
Who wins
US and allied clouds with no Chinese tenants; Huawei and other domestic Chinese chipmakers inside China.
Who loses
Offshore hubs serving Chinese labs; Megaspeed; NVIDIA's international cloud partners.

4. Anthropic's listing draws orders several times the shares on offer.

Bloomberg reported Wednesday that Anthropic's initial public offering (IPO) is multiple times oversubscribed on anchor-size orders. The company seeks up to $90.4 billion from the offering and a supplier placement, at a fully diluted value of about $1.4 trillion, and posted its offering announcement on X on Thursday.

+Bull

Demand held through the chip selloff and NVIDIA's leadership crisis, so public markets are willing to fund a lab's compute bill at trillion-dollar scale.

−Bear

The value sits below the roughly $1.5 trillion expected a week earlier, some buyers are passing on allocations for fees, and a reported plan not to extend the SpaceX compute contract is unconfirmed.

My call (Likely (Bloomberg via Quartz and Bloomberg Law; unnamed sources))
Anthropic prices at or near the top of its $70 to $78 range (Guessing). A strong debut sets the cost of capital for every lab that lists after it.
Who wins
Anthropic; its seven supplier buyers (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC); lead banks Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley.
Who loses
OpenAI's private round, which now competes with a rival stock investors can trade.

5. OpenAI's revenue grows 50 percent, and so does its loss.

OpenAI told investors on Tuesday that third-quarter revenue rose about 50 percent from the second quarter to about $10 billion, with an adjusted operating loss of $10.9 billion versus $10.1 billion and about 100 million paying ChatGPT users, The Wall Street Journal reported.

+Bull

Revenue growth at that scale is rare, and president Greg Brockman says OpenAI will be ready to go public in 2027.

−Bear

Anthropic reported $16.9 billion of third-quarter revenue with a $567 million operating loss, so OpenAI loses about a dollar for every dollar of sales while its rival sits near breakeven.

My call (Likely (The Wall Street Journal via MarketWatch; OpenAI did not comment); Certain on Anthropic's figures (its filing))
Anthropic's listing will make OpenAI's loss the most-watched number in the industry. Expect pressure on OpenAI to show compute discipline before its own offering.
Who wins
Anthropic's investor pitch; compute suppliers, who are paid either way.
Who loses
OpenAI's pricing power in its current round.

06|Unknown knowns
Public facts the market is underweighting. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

About a third of Nebius's quarter was one-time revenue

About $325 million of Nebius's $980.8 million third-quarter revenue was non-recurring, from the first phase of its asset-light partnership model. Without it, revenue would have been roughly $656 million against a $907.5 million consensus. Headlines called it a beat; the recurring business came in below expectations.

Confidence: Certain on the figures (Blockspace, citing the company and BofA); Likely on the subtraction (our arithmetic)

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Nebius's breakeven assumes chips last five years

Nebius moved server and network equipment to a five-year depreciation life from four years in 2026, and its pre-tax breakeven target for the fourth quarter of 2027 assumes that five-year life. Longer lives lower yearly depreciation and lift reported profit. If graphics processing units (GPUs) lose value faster as new generations ship, breakeven slides out.

Confidence: Certain (Nebius shareholder letter, via Blockspace)

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Open models carry 19 percent of enterprise tokens for 4 percent of spend

Menlo Ventures' 2026 State of Enterprise AI report says open-source models are 4 percent of US enterprise model spending but handle 19 percent of tokens, and Chinese models take 89 percent of that open-source spend: Moonshot's Kimi 61 percent, DeepSeek 15 percent, Zhipu's GLM 11 percent, and Alibaba's Qwen 3 percent. Coverage led with the China share; the price gap is the bigger signal, since enterprises are moving real work to models that cost a fraction as much per token.

Confidence: Likely (Menlo Ventures report, as relayed by Dealroom and DigiTimes)

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Anthropic's $120.5 billion SpaceX deal is a ceiling with a 90-day exit

Posts on Wednesday said Anthropic told sell-side analysts its SpaceX compute contract will not be extended past mid-2027; neither company has confirmed it. Readings of Anthropic's filing say the $120.5 billion is a maximum through 2029, and that after non-cancelable periods ending in January and March 2027 either side can exit with 90 days' notice. The biggest compute contracts are less fixed than their headline totals.

Confidence: Likely on the contract terms (secondary readings of the filing by TeslaNorth and Tenbrief); Guessing on Anthropic's intent

07|Unknown unknowns
Surprises from outside the big players. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

Runway gives away a robot brain that learns from video

Runway released Praxis-1 on Wednesday, a 27 billion parameter world action model for robots, with open weights plus inference and fine-tuning code under the Apache 2.0 license. Runway says it reached 46.2 percent success on NVIDIA's RoboLab-120 benchmark, ahead of the other open policies it listed, and learned a warehouse plank-picking task from half an hour of examples. Runway's research says web video works as well as teleoperated robot video for pretraining, which moves the limit on robot skill from collecting robot data to how much video a model can train on, a new source of compute demand.

Confidence: Likely (Runway via The Robot Report; self-reported benchmarks)

Read more on GrokSee it on X

SK hynix reportedly pushes HBM4 yield above 70 percent

Korean media reported Thursday that SK hynix's production yield on HBM4, the high-bandwidth memory stacked next to new AI accelerators, has topped 70 percent. The company has not disclosed the number but said on October 28 that HBM4 yields beat internal targets. Higher yield means more usable memory per wafer, which can loosen the memory limit on accelerator shipments in 2027, even as memory stocks fell about 4 percent premarket with NVIDIA.

Confidence: Likely (Chosun Biz; the company confirmed only that targets were beaten; stock move from Yahoo Finance)
