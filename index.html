02|The Big Three

Anthropic's $895 billion compute bill

What it is: Anthropic's amended IPO filing showed about $17 billion of third-quarter revenue, up 47 percent from the second quarter, an operating loss of about half a billion dollars, and about $895.4 billion of purchase and lease commitments, substantially all for compute. The Wall Street Journal reported IPO investors want a $1.5 to $1.6 trillion valuation, below the roughly $2 trillion discussed earlier.

Why it matters: This is compute demand written into a legal filing, and the company says it paid a $5 billion premium for short-term capacity because demand outran its contracts. Investors still want a discount, because growth is slowing and the commitments are almost seven times its cash.

Oracle pays up to keep building

What it is: Oracle priced $10 billion of new stock at $116, an 11.2 percent discount to Friday's close, and $10 billion of bonds with coupons from 7.000 to 8.650 percent, maturing 2029 to 2046. The shares fell 8.8 percent Monday, Reuters reported, and both deals close November 4.

Why it matters: An investment-grade cloud company paying 8 percent on 11-year debt sets the price of AI money for everyone below the top credits, including neoclouds. Funding is still there, but at a clearly higher price.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

NVIDIA rations who can rent its chips

What it is: NVIDIA told cloud partners worldwide that at least 51 percent of their services must go to non-Chinese companies, the FT reported, citing five people. The rule was delivered verbally and covers only future contracts; NVIDIA neither confirmed nor denied it.

Why it matters: Chinese labs have rented NVIDIA capacity abroad to get around export limits, and some overseas clouds depend on them. A chip supplier is now deciding who can buy compute, ahead of any formal rule.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

HD Hyundai's robot agents cut problem-fixing time 63 percent

Who and what: HD Hyundai Robotics, the Korean industrial robot maker, uses AI agents built on Palantir's Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) to analyze existing robot programs and propose fixes, which engineers then review and refine. Results were presented at Palantir's Sovereignty Bootcamp in New York. Model: not disclosed. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Average problem-resolution time fell from 21.5 days to 7.9 days, a 63 percent cut, since the agents went live in August 2026. The company targets a more than 50 percent cut in robot programming costs. Tag: Likely (company-reported, no outside audit).

Strength and weakness: Strength: the agents work from years of structured program history, and engineers keep the final say. Weakness: results depend on that stored history, so a newcomer without it cannot copy them quickly, and the cost target is a goal, not a result.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed. Live since August 2026, about three months.

Maturity: Production.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Circle K's alert agents cut incident triage to under 15 minutes

Who and what: Circle K, the convenience store chain, built an automated incident triage system on Amazon Bedrock. When a monitoring alert fires, two agents check its Amazon Web Services (AWS) resources and its logs in parallel, a third searches its code and recent changes on GitHub, and a final model call posts a likely root cause, evidence, owner, and suggested fix to Slack. Model: not disclosed. Chips and cloud: AWS; chips not disclosed.

Measured result: Mean triage time fell from 45 to 60 minutes to under 15 minutes, a 67 to 75 percent cut, by Circle K's own incident metrics. Tag: Likely (AWS blog co-written with a Circle K engineer; no sample size or accuracy rate published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: automates the repetitive log searches and runbook lookups at the start of every incident, while engineers still do the fix. Weakness: no published hit rate on root causes, and some incidents outgrew the first design, prompting a move to Bedrock AgentCore.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

4. Power is now a ballot question and a real estate play.

Brookfield and a venture backed by the Qatar Investment Authority agreed to invest 2.5 billion pounds in the first phase of a data center at the Drax power station in North Yorkshire, targeting up to 100 megawatts and operation in the first quarter of 2028, 500 megawatts by 2031, and more than one gigawatt later, with total investment that could exceed 20 billion pounds, subject to planning and grid approvals. In Texas, the grid operator's interconnection queue holds about 474 gigawatts, roughly 90 percent data centers and more than five times the state's record peak, and the governor's office has ordered an audit of data center grid requests. Voters go to the polls today.

+Bull

Building next to existing power stations skips the grid queue, and investors are paying to do it.

−Bear

Drax's first phase is only 100 megawatts in 2028, a rounding error against announced demand, and Texas has paused new approvals pending its audit.

My call (Certain on Drax and the Texas audit, Likely on the queue figure)
Sites with power already connected are the scarcest asset in AI. The queue numbers mostly measure demand that cannot connect this decade.
Who wins
Power station owners and investors with patient capital.
Who loses
Projects waiting in grid queues (Likely).

5. Graphics processor rental prices are rising, not falling.

Nebius removed the ceiling on spot prices for its preemptible virtual machines on November 2, so spot rates for graphics processing units (GPUs) can now exceed the on-demand rate when demand is high. Anthropic's filing shows it paid a $5 billion charge for short-term compute that currently costs significantly more than its long-term deals. Matt Garman, chief executive of Amazon Web Services (AWS), posted that AWS has lots of capacity, a $184 billion run rate, and a $578 billion committed backlog.

+Bull

Pricing power at the margin shows demand still outruns supply, which supports neocloud revenue and lenders.

−Bear

AWS says it has room, so customers who can move will shop the largest cloud against neocloud spot prices.

My call (Certain on Nebius and the filing, Likely on the read)
Tripwire 4, a market-wide fall in GPU rental prices, moved further away this window.
Who wins
Neoclouds with spare capacity and the lenders behind them.
Who loses
Startups that rent compute by the hour (Likely).

About $214 billion of Anthropic's commitments can be cancelled or depend on conditions

Euronews and DatacenterDynamics, reading the amended filing, report that about $214 billion of the $895.4 billion can be cancelled or depends on conditions being met. The SpaceX deal, $120.5 billion, can be cancelled on 90 days' notice from next March; Nscale must first obtain financing for its NVIDIA GPUs, and Lambda must secure its facility lease. The Google, Amazon, and Microsoft deals are mostly non-cancelable. The headline number overstates how much compute is locked in, and the soft part sits with the neoclouds.

Confidence: Certain on the filing details as reported, Likely on the read

Apple says AI memory demand is now hitting phone margins

Apple guided holiday-quarter gross margin to 45 to 46 percent, down from 47.9 percent in the September quarter, and chief executive John Ternus called memory pricing unprecedented. Reuters reported Apple expects memory costs to keep rising and has locked in supply; CNBC reported purchasing commitments rose by $33 billion. The memory makers' sold-out story is a cost story for every device maker.

07|Unknown unknowns
Surprises from outside the big players. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

A frontier model edited a small model's weights directly and lifted its math score sixfold

MIT researchers reported that GPT-6 Astra read and rewrote the open weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B without any training run, changing 131,072 of about 0.8 billion weights, or 0.016 percent, in two layers. Scores rose from 7.9 to 48.1 percent on MATH-500, a math test, and from 13.8 to 67.1 percent on GSM8K, a grade-school math test. The edit mostly removed a repetitive reasoning loop rather than adding knowledge. If the method generalizes, some model improvement could happen without new training compute.

Confidence: Likely on the result (research blog, not peer reviewed), Guessing on how far it generalizes

11|State of the Thesis
Thesis status: Unchanged (Nov 3, 2026)
The belief: labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work, demand for compute keeps compounding, and the bottleneck moves to power, memory, and who finances the chips. Nothing changed the shape of any part this window. Anthropic's filing put $895.4 billion of compute commitments on the record, Oracle paid up to 8.65 percent to keep building, Apple flagged memory costs, and NVIDIA began rationing who can rent its chips. OpenAI invited an outside red team to attack its defenses.

Unchanged means nothing tested it. Revised means new evidence reshaped one part. Rewritten means a falsifier actually happened.

1. Labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work
Frontier labs keep releasing new models while regulators and outside researchers check their work, rather than pausing on their own. Key evidence: OpenAI brought in an outside red team, Cathedral Systems, to attack its defenses with OpenAI's own models, and more than quadrupled bug bounty rewards for critical findings, up to about $172,000 with bonuses. Supporting note: Recursive self-improvement watch: MIT researchers had GPT-6 Astra edit 0.016 percent of a small open model's weights directly and lift its MATH-500 score from 7.9 to 48.1 percent, and commentators relay that Anthropic's filing describes each Claude model helping build the next (Guessing on significance).
Unchanged
2. Demand for compute keeps compounding
Buyers keep needing more computing power, and cheaper models so far add to demand instead of cutting it. Key evidence: Anthropic's filing shows about $17 billion of quarterly revenue and $895.4 billion of compute commitments, plus $5 billion of premium-priced short-term compute; Nebius uncapped spot prices; AWS cites a $578 billion backlog. Supporting note: Growth is slowing, from 144 percent to 47 percent quarter over quarter, and about $214 billion of the commitments are cancelable or conditional (Certain on the numbers, Likely on the read).
Unchanged
3. The bottleneck moves to power, memory, and who finances the chips
Chips alone no longer set the limit. Electricity, memory supply, and who is willing to finance the hardware decide who ships. Key evidence: Oracle paid up to 8.65 percent on new debt and sold stock at an 11 percent discount; Anthropic's IPO buyers want a lower price; Apple says memory costs hit its margins; Texas holds a 474 gigawatt queue under audit. Supporting note: Still the strongest part. NVIDIA's 49 percent China cap adds a policy layer to who can rent compute. No tripwire moved: Oracle was not downgraded and the Nscale guarantee is still unsigned.
Unchanged
