02|The Big Three

Anthropic prices the largest IPO on record at $83

What it is: Anthropic priced 980 million Class A shares at $83 on Wednesday, above its $70 to $78 range, raising $81.34 billion, ahead of SpaceX's $75 billion initial raise in June. A concurrent $26.0 billion private placement of Class N shares sold at the same price, and underwriters can buy 147 million more shares. Trading on Nasdaq as ANT begins Thursday, at a fully diluted value of about $1.5 trillion (CNBC). Sources told CNBC that retail demand was the largest for any deal in history and the stock could trade into the low $100s. The prospectus shows $33.24 billion of revenue for the nine months to September 30, up about 1,200 percent, and run-rate revenue near $81 billion at the end of October.

Why it matters: The largest buyer of compute outside the hyperscalers now has public money to pay suppliers, many of whom also bought its stock. The financing question shifts from whether labs can raise to whether revenue keeps pace with what they have committed to spend.

Control check: If public investors are truly underwriting frontier compute, other AI financings should get easier the same week, and they did not on Wednesday: CoreWeave fell 4.13 percent and NVIDIA 2.89 percent as NVIDIA stressed disciplined neocloud support (CNBC), so the money reached the lab, not yet the clouds that rent to it (Likely).

OpenAI's models redesign AMD's GPU and OpenAI's own chip

What it is: OpenAI said Wednesday that its models rewrote the code that describes chip circuits and found up to 14 percent improvement in power, performance, and area (PPA) against heavily optimized baselines on AMD's flagship graphics processing unit (GPU) design, in under five months, roughly a year of normal progress. AMD has put some changes into a recent design sent for manufacturing and verified single-digit gains in key blocks of its next flagship central processing unit (CPU). In OpenAI's own Jalapeno inference chip, built with Broadcom, model-proposed changes cut circuit area about 13 percent in the vector unit and 10 percent in the matrix unit, proven on real silicon. OpenAI is also training GPT-Synopsys to use Synopsys electronic design automation (EDA) tools the way expert designers do.

Why it matters: This is the clearest public case yet of AI improving the hardware that runs AI. Faster design cycles compress chip roadmaps and reward the designers and tool vendors that adopt first.

Control check: If the 14 percent is real, AMD should confirm it in its own words and a shipping product should show it; AMD's account quoted Greg Brockman and some changes went into a design sent for manufacturing, but no AMD-authored figures, product name, or split across power, speed, and area has been published (Likely).

Self-improvement becomes the fight on IPO day

What it is: On Wednesday morning, the Center for AI Safety (CAIS) published the MisAnthropic Statement, signed by figures including Steven Pinker, Nobel laureate Oliver Hart, Turing Award winner Judea Pearl, Hugging Face's Thomas Wolf, accelerationist Guillaume Verdon, Max Tegmark, and George Hotz. The statement says Anthropic "warns that recursively self-improving AI could concentrate power and escape human control, yet races to build it first." David Sacks called it an Anthropic-Doomer split. That afternoon OpenAI published research acceleration data: agents now finish 46 percent of research tasks that take a person two to four hours, up from 23 percent in March, and 35 percent of four-to-eight-hour tasks, up from 13 percent, while humans remain central. OpenAI said labs should be required to track and publicly report indicators of recursive self-improvement (RSI). At the Nasdaq on Thursday, Dario Amodei renewed Anthropic's safety pledge (CNBC).

Why it matters: Self-improvement has moved from a safety paper term to a selling point investors price and a policy hook rivals use against each other. Mandatory reporting would turn each lab's self-measurement into a compliance regime, which favors the labs that already publish.

Control check: If AI is really speeding up research, it should show up outside OpenAI too, as faster releases and more output per researcher at other labs; Anthropic's filing says it met its own research acceleration threshold, but no outside auditor has verified either lab's figures, and OpenAI's own task results are uneven, from 79 percent on monitoring training runs to 25 percent on serving code (Likely).

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

CommBank turns threat intelligence into action in five minutes, down from four hours

Who and what: Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CommBank) runs Agentic Shield, five specialized AI agents in its security operations center: one hunts for suspicious activity, one gathers context, and others document incidents, check investigations for misses, and look for gaps in detection. Human analysts decide what action to take. The bank described it at its Accelerate AI conference in Sydney on November 18 and released CodeCheck, a free AI tool for businesses to find software flaws. Model, chips, and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Time to turn threat intelligence into action fell from four hours to five minutes, while the bank now analyzes more than 265 billion signals a week, up 33 percent in a year. Tag: Likely (company-reported, no method published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: a team of narrow agents fits how security work is already split, and it lets defenders keep pace with attackers whose fastest recorded breakout is 27 seconds (CrowdStrike, cited by CommBank). Weakness: humans still own every decision, and the bank says it slowed some business development to speed up cyber work, a real cost the figure does not show.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

AMD engineers ship circuit changes written by OpenAI's models

Who and what: AMD's chip designers worked with OpenAI's frontier models to rewrite the SystemVerilog code that describes circuits in AMD's flagship GPU design and key blocks of its next flagship CPU, with Synopsys tools checking that each rewrite kept the same behavior. OpenAI used the same approach on its own Jalapeno inference chip, starting with an early reasoning model in 2025. Chips and cloud used to run the models: not disclosed.

Measured result: Up to 14 percent PPA improvement on the GPU design in under five months, single-digit gains in CPU blocks verified by AMD, and about 13 and 10 percent less circuit area in two Jalapeno units, proven in silicon. Tag: Likely (OpenAI-reported; AMD endorsed it publicly but published no figures of its own).

Strength and weakness: Strength: gains on top of designs AMD had optimized for years, with changes already in a design sent for manufacturing. Weakness: 14 percent is a ceiling with no breakdown, OpenAI notes limits in its own chip design tests, and engineers still review and verify every change.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed; about five months of joint work.

Maturity: Production (in a design sent for manufacturing).

5. Washington weighs making companies disclose Chinese AI models.

Reuters reported Wednesday that Treasury and White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) officials have discussed requiring US companies to disclose on their websites when they use Chinese AI models, including open-weight models such as Alibaba's Qwen. No rule, deadline, or penalty has been announced, and supporters stress it is not a ban.

+Bull

Buyers and customers get to see which models sit behind a service, and a label costs far less than a ban for companies that rely on cheap open models.

−Bear

A label says nothing about where data goes (Tech Times), and open-weight models already carry about 31 percent of the enterprise tokens Databricks tracks, so the paperwork lands on many companies at once.

My call (Likely (Reuters, officials familiar; no proposal text))
If a rule comes, it starts with federal contractors and widens later (Guessing).
Who wins
US open-weight builders such as Thinking Machines and OpenAI's open models; compliance vendors.
Who loses
Inference platforms and apps built on Qwen, DeepSeek, or Kimi.

OpenAI researchers would give up 40 percent of their team to keep coding agents

Buried in OpenAI's research acceleration report: asked what they would trade to keep frontier coding agents for three months, the median researcher said 30 percent of team compute or, separately, 40 percent of team headcount. The median researcher's daily agent use reached $2,400 at API list prices in October, up 62 percent from September, and $23,000 at the 90th percentile. At list prices, $2,400 a working day is roughly $600,000 a year per researcher, more than many salaries. Headlines covered self-improvement; the quieter signal is that agent spending now competes with hiring inside the labs.

Confidence: Certain on the survey and usage figures (OpenAI); Guessing on the annual cost, which assumes about 250 working days at list prices

Memory makers have sold 2027 and are now selling 2028

Micron said at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin on Wednesday that calendar 2027 and 2028 supply will be even tighter than 2026. On its October 1 call, chief executive Sanjay Mehrotra said more than 75 percent of 2027 output was already committed and most customer talks are about 2028, with some agreements running into 2031 and new cleanroom capacity not arriving until late 2028. Any lab or cloud that has not signed memory for 2027 is buying from what is left.

Alibaba keeps its new flagship Qwen4-Max closed

Alibaba launched Qwen4-Max this week only through its hosted cloud application programming interface (API), at $1.50 per million input tokens, and said smaller Qwen4 models will get open weights later, with no date. Chinese labs have led the open-weight price war, and DesignArena found open models caught 6 of 11 flagship releases on its agentic board after 85 days on average. If the strongest Chinese labs start holding back their best weights, the cheap open model supply that pushed prices down slows just as Washington weighs disclosure rules for Chinese models.

Confidence: Likely (Alibaba; The Next Web; DesignArena); Guessing on the industry effect

Blackwell fingerprints found inside DeepSeek and Kimi weights

Former OpenAI researcher Will DePue reported that DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Moonshot's Kimi K3 carry initialization patterns that match Blackwell-class GPU layouts of 148 and 152 streaming multiprocessors, and independent replications, including one published by the Institute for Progress, reproduced the match. The evidence supports access to export-controlled NVIDIA hardware, but not where the chips sat or how they were obtained. A forensic method for tracing training hardware from released weights is new, and could become an export-control enforcement tool.

Confidence: Likely on the replication (The Decoder; GitHub replications); Guessing on enforcement use

11|State of the Thesis
Thesis status: Unchanged (Nov 19, 2026)
The belief: labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work, demand for compute keeps compounding, and the bottleneck moves to power, memory, who finances the chips, and who has permission to build and to rent. Status is Unchanged: Part 3 keeps the Nov 4 Revised wording. Part 1 held as outside checks moved from critics toward proposed mandatory reporting of self-improvement. Part 2 held as public investors put $81.34 billion into Anthropic and OpenAI's researchers kept raising agent spend. Part 3 held with a twist: equity eased financing for one lab while neocloud shares fell, and Micron says memory gets tighter through 2028. AI found chip design gains for AMD, but that speeds the loop rather than breaking any part, so no falsifier happened.

Unchanged means nothing tested it. Revised means new evidence reshaped one part. Rewritten means a falsifier actually happened.

1. Labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work
Frontier labs keep releasing new models while regulators and outside researchers check their work, rather than pausing on their own. Key evidence: OpenAI published research acceleration data and said labs should be required to report self-improvement indicators (Certain); the Center for AI Safety's MisAnthropic Statement, signed across safety, open-source, and accelerationist camps, attacked Anthropic's race to self-improvement on pricing day (Certain); Dario Amodei renewed Anthropic's safety pledge at the Nasdaq (Certain, CNBC). Supporting note: Recursive self-improvement (RSI) note: OpenAI says agents now finish 46 percent of two-to-four-hour research tasks, up from 23 percent in March, while humans remain central. Checks are moving from outside critics to proposed mandatory reporting. No lab has announced a pace cap (Tripwire 2 clear), and METR has published no Critical rating (Falsifier B clear).
Unchanged
2. Demand for compute keeps compounding
Buyers keep needing more computing power, and cheaper models so far add to demand instead of cutting it. Key evidence: Anthropic raised $81.34 billion at $83 with run-rate revenue near $81 billion (Certain on the raise; Likely on run-rate, CNBC); OpenAI's median researcher used $2,400 a day of agents in October, up 62 percent in a month (Certain); Apollo Hospitals and CommBank reported production agents (Likely). Supporting note: Open-weight pressure: DesignArena says open models caught 6 of 11 flagship releases after 85 days on average, while Alibaba kept Qwen4-Max closed (Likely). No market-wide GPU rental price cut (Tripwire 4 clear).
Unchanged
3. The bottleneck moves to power, memory, and who finances the chips
Chips alone no longer set the limit. Electricity, memory supply, who will finance the hardware, and now who has permission to build and to rent decide who ships. Key evidence: Equity eased the financing limit for one lab, with $107 billion raised at $83 including suppliers' placement (Certain); the neoclouds did not share it, as CoreWeave fell 4.13 percent (Certain, CNBC); Micron says 2027 and 2028 memory supply will be tighter than 2026 (Likely). Supporting note: Part 3 wording stays as Revised on Nov 4. Financing loosened for labs and tightened for the clouds that rent to them; memory stays the hardest limit. No tripwire falsified.
