02|The Big Three

OpenAI ships GPT-6 Cyber as a Critical cyber model

What it is: On November 5 OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Cyber, trained to find and validate exploitable vulnerabilities within researcher-set scope. ExploitGym rose to 48.5 percent from 32.1 percent for GPT-5.6 Cyber. OpenAI classifies the model Critical for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework, up from High. Researchers used it for 948 Linux local privilege escalations and 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities; Mozilla began landing fixes. Named users include Mozilla, Calif, Cognition, Palo Alto Networks, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Perplexity, and CodeRabbit. Access starts with select Daybreak Red customers. Alignment tests reported 99.93 percent in-scope without the deployment prompt and 100 percent with it.

Why it matters: Labs are productizing a contained Critical-capability cyber agent for trusted defenders rather than releasing it broadly. Part 1 of the thesis gets a Preparedness rating and a customer list, not only a research write-up.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Anthropic's $1.5 trillion IPO meets investor pushback

What it is: The Wall Street Journal reported that major institutions are skeptical Anthropic can complete an initial public offering at a $1.5 trillion valuation after the AI equity selloff. The company had targeted the week before Thanksgiving to raise up to $100 billion. One investor suggested pricing closer to $1 trillion; backers are debating a cut or a delay. Cash stood at $146.6 billion as of September 30. Journal relays put Thursday's Nasdaq Composite market-value loss near $2.5 trillion.

Why it matters: Tripwire 1 on Anthropic's IPO timing, price, and rating is live. Lab financing is now priced like data center financing: permission and power risk show up in private rounds too.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Microsoft tightens neocloud oversight as AI equities crater

What it is: Bloomberg via Reuters reported Microsoft tightened oversight of Nebius, IREN, and Nscale projects, is stepping away from builds it deems risky, and is debating new neocloud deals while prioritizing its own data centers. Nebius fell about 14 percent, IREN about 17 percent, and CoreWeave about 10 percent Thursday. IREN is pushing some Microsoft delivery dates after network design changes without penalties. Microsoft will not more than double its Texas Nscale deal and weeks ago pulled out of anchoring Nscale's Loughton, United Kingdom site after power delays into the 2030s. CNBC said the Nasdaq fell 5.5 percent, its worst day since April 2025, with Nvidia and Qualcomm each down more than 9 percent and Nvidia losing nearly $450 billion of market value.

Why it matters: The hyperscaler customer is now a bottleneck for neocloud expansion. Permission politics plus buyer pullbacks ration new capacity even when chip demand still prints.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

Mozilla lands patches after GPT-6 Cyber finds 81 novel Firefox flaws

Who and what: OpenAI security researchers ran GPT-6 Cyber against the Firefox codebase with Mozilla's engagement. Mozilla Distinguished Engineer Brian Grinstead is quoted in OpenAI's November 5 launch post. Model: GPT-6 Cyber. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Eighty-one previously unknown high-severity vulnerabilities with reproducible tests; thirty known issues shown more severe than reported; Mozilla began landing fixes. Tag: Certain (OpenAI primary post with Mozilla quote).

Strength and weakness: Strength: defender-scale discovery on a hardened browser after prior frontier-model hardening. Weakness: dual-use model limited to Daybreak Red; findings are company-reported.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed for the Firefox campaign. The related Linux local privilege escalation run cost $1,213,430 in API credits.

Maturity: Production.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Caterpillar trains construction autonomy on CoreWeave with NVIDIA

Who and what: Caterpillar works with CoreWeave and NVIDIA on physical AI for autonomous construction machines, with the partnership starting in 2026. CoreWeave published the case study and promoted it in the Nov 5 window.

Measured result: Development cycles that once took years are now measured in months. For every hour of real machine data, Caterpillar expects roughly 1,000 simulated hours. Tag: Likely (company case study; no independent audit).

Strength and weakness: Strength: full loop of data prep, simulation, training, and evals on GPU capacity. Weakness: no published accuracy, safety, or dollar savings figures.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Moloco runs 650 billion daily inferences on Google's XLA Serving Engine

Who and what: Moloco's demand-side platform uses Google's newly open-sourced XLA Serving Engine (XSE) for C++ JAX serving, complementing Cloud TPU Trillium. Google announced XSE on November 5 under Apache 2.0.

Measured result: Moloco processes over 650 billion daily inferences and peaks at 10.5 million queries per second. Google says XSE already serves over 6 million queries per second internally across YouTube Shorts, Ads, Search, and parts of Gemini Omni, with studies claiming 2 to 5 times the throughput of the best Python server tested. Tag: Certain (Google Open Source Blog quotes).

Strength and weakness: Strength: train-in-Python, serve-in-C++ for high-QPS non-LLM workloads. Weakness: Google says long-context autoregressive language models should use vLLM or SGLang instead.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: At scale.

Export controls on shipping chips do not equal controls on renting them abroad, yet.

European neoclouds willing to take Chinese offtake at a premium.

5. Google open-sources XLA Serving Engine; Arm tunes Neoverse including Axion.

Google open-sourced XLA Serving Engine for train-in-Python, serve-in-C++ inference under Apache 2.0. Google says XSE serves over 6 million queries per second internally. Moloco said it processes over 650 billion daily inferences and peaks at 10.5 million QPS. Arm's Ian Bratt said the collaboration optimized the XLA CPU backend for vision, embedding, and recommendation workloads on Arm Neoverse platforms including Google Cloud Axion.

+Bull

High-QPS non-LLM inference gets a production C++ path on Arm and x86 hosts.

−Bear

Google says long-context autoregressive LLMs should stay on vLLM or SGLang, so this is not a frontier-LLM runtime story.

My call (Certain (Google Open Source Blog))
Inference software optimization is another way demand compounds without new racks.
Who wins
Arm Neoverse and Axion hosts; Moloco-style ad and recommender stacks; OpenXLA ecosystem.
Who loses
Pure-Python serving stacks for short-request high-QPS workloads.
