02|The Big Three

CoreWeave beats, and customers start paying up front

What it is: CoreWeave reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion on Tuesday, up 168 percent and above guidance, with adjusted operating income of $379 million against a $200 million to $260 million guide, a revenue backlog of $132.5 billion (up from $104 billion in June), and more than 1.7 gigawatts of active power. Management said it will collect about $9 billion of customer prepayments and deposits in the next three months, including more than $3 billion from recontracting older capacity at premiums; 70 percent of this year's deals carried prepayments, some funding up to 100 percent of contract value up front. CoreWeave raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion and does not intend to issue new parent-level unsecured debt at current yields. The net loss was $709 million on $846 million of interest expense.

Why it matters: Graphics processing unit (GPU) clouds are changing how they get financed: buyers, not bond investors, now carry more of the bill, just as the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5 percent after a hot October inflation report. Chief executive Michael Intrator said qualified demand is ten times available capacity through 2027 and that state pushback relocates data centers rather than reducing demand (Likely, relayed on X).

Anthropic says AI now speeds up its own research enough to trip its safety rule

What it is: In the November 10 filing that set its listing range, Anthropic wrote that it has met the AI research and development capability threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy, the company rulebook that adds safeguards as models grow more powerful. The threshold covers AI-driven progress roughly twice as fast as before, about two years of 2018 to 2024 progress packed into one. Anthropic said the call rests on what it expects from future models and its overall rate of progress, not on what current models have shown, that it continues to assess the data, and that it will publish further reports. One week earlier its first amendment said the threshold might be met imminently, and METR, an outside evaluator, estimated on November 5 that Anthropic's progress had reached about 1.9 times its earlier rate.

Why it matters: A frontier lab told public-market investors that AI is now speeding up its own research, the clearest recursive self-improvement signal yet, eight days before pricing. Anthropic says crossing the line means more safeguards, not a shutdown, and that humans stay in control; no pace cap was announced, so Tripwire 2 stays clear.

A report ties 240,000 NVIDIA chips in China to the Huang affair

What it is: The Information reported Tuesday, citing two people who know her, that Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US authorities reportedly view as a Chinese intelligence asset, used her relationship with Jensen Huang to help move about 240,000 restricted H100, H200, and some A100 chips, worth at least $7.5 billion, into China. The hub was Shanghai Century Litong, founded in June 2023 and named an NVIDIA preferred cloud partner by October 2023, whose capacity served Alibaba, Tencent, and smaller labs. Reuters could not independently verify the report; Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and NVIDIA has said its own review of Megaspeed found no diversion.

Why it matters: The NVIDIA story moved from governance to export enforcement, which reaches distributors, cloud partners, and the White House AI team. Jensen Huang faces a closed-door House briefing on November 18, the same day Anthropic prices, while AMD and Google's custom chips gain from buyers hedging.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

SoftBank's AI scan finds 35,000 more security flaws after fixing 10,500

Who and what: SoftBank Corp., the Japanese carrier, ran OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra over its internally built systems after patching 10,500 flaws found in a June review of about 700 systems; President Junichi Miyakawa disclosed the result at the November 9 results briefing. SoftBank also sells the approach as Patching as a Service, built on OpenAI technology, with 7,670 contracted companies as of October 30. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: About 35,000 additional vulnerabilities found, more than three times the June count; assessment scope widened from about 700 to more than 1,800 systems after a ransomware attack on subsidiary IDC Frontier that took seven minutes. Tag: Likely (executive remarks reported by CNET Japan and Asia Daily; no raw data).

Strength and weakness: Strength: each newer model finds flaws older scans missed, and patch application is automated. Weakness: checking that patched systems still work is manual, there is no completion date for the 35,000 fixes, and the IDC Frontier entry point was open source software left out of the earlier scan.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

Singapore Airlines doubles chatbot satisfaction with OpenAI models

Who and what: Singapore Airlines moved its Kris customer service chatbot from scripted dialog flows to the OpenAI API, added call summaries for service agents, runs a natural-language Flight Recommender, and gives staff OpenAI models through its internal Jarvis platform, per an OpenAI case study published November 10. Model versions, chips, and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Customer satisfaction score (CSAT) for Kris doubled; average handling time for service agents fell about 10 percent; Flight Recommender monthly users grew about 3 times in a year; selected software lifecycle tasks gained up to 20 percent in productivity; more than 60 percent of employees use Jarvis monthly. Tag: Likely (vendor case study; no baselines published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: broad, measured deployment across customers, agents, and engineers, with guardrails and human review. Weakness: the numbers are relative and vendor-published, and complex transactions such as booking changes are still being added.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: At scale.

Treasury agents on Amazon Bedrock cut reporting from days to under a minute

Who and what: Trovata, a treasury software company, runs autonomous agents for cash forecasting, anomaly detection, and reconciliation on Amazon Bedrock and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, and offers them through banks under their own brands, per an Amazon Web Services post on November 10. Model and chips: not disclosed.

Measured result: Customers report more than $1 million of annualized savings, more than five hours saved per accounting team each day, a 77 percent cut in treasury management system costs, and reporting across hundreds of bank accounts in under one minute instead of hours or days. Tag: Likely (vendor-reported; customers not named).

Strength and weakness: Strength: ten production agent patterns with role-based access controls in a regulated field. Weakness: no named customer, sample size, or error rate, so the savings cannot be checked.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed beyond the savings claims.

1. CoreWeave beats, and customers start paying up front: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

Management sees ten times more qualified demand than capacity through 2027, and customers funding up to 100 percent of a contract up front make the buildout largely self-financing.

−Bear

Net interest expense of $846 million was four times operating income of $207 million, and a 10-year Treasury yield near 5 percent raises the cost of every refinancing.

My call (Certain on results and prepayments (CoreWeave release and call); Likely on the demand multiple (management estimate))
Neocloud risk is moving from lenders to the biggest customers, which is healthier for CoreWeave and riskier for the labs that prepay.
Who wins
CoreWeave; NVIDIA, whose GPUs fill the backlog; owners of older GPU fleets now recontracting at higher prices.
Who loses
Private credit funds losing a large borrower; labs tying up cash in prepayments ahead of their own listings.

2. Anthropic says AI now speeds up its own research enough to trip its safety rule: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

A lab declaring the line crossed, in a securities filing and before any regulator forced it, is checkable accountability, and the step adds safeguards rather than stopping work.

Anthropic released Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta and made Claude Data generally available on all paid plans, with Claude Meetings in beta for Team and Enterprise customers. Microsoft shares fell about 2 to 3 percent on the news.

5. SoftBank writes off $3.7 billion of US power equipment bets.

SoftBank swung to a 1.12 trillion yen (about $7.1 billion) quarterly loss, including $3.7 billion on advance payments and contract changes for power-generation equipment it no longer plans to buy for US AI data centers. Of $9.5 billion paid so far, $6.6 billion is reallocated to the PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio and other projects, where OpenAI holds a 20-year, 8 gigawatt lease.

+Bull

SoftBank bought turbines and grid gear early to beat shortages, kept most of it, and holds rebate rights of up to $2.1 billion; the Ohio lease is intact.

−Bear

Even the most aggressive buyer overbought power equipment, a sign that site plans shift faster than heavy gear can be ordered or cancelled.

My call (Certain (SoftBank investor briefing; CNBC))
Power equipment is now a balance-sheet risk, not only a bottleneck.
Who wins
Turbine and grid suppliers that kept the deposits; Siemens Energy rose about 7 percent on AI power demand (Likely, market relay).
Who loses
SoftBank's near-term earnings; projects dropped from its revised plan.

07|Unknown unknowns
Surprises from outside the big players. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

Meituan gives away a 1.6 trillion parameter model built on Chinese chips

Meituan, a food-delivery company, released LongCat-2.5 weights under the MIT license on Monday night: a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with about 48 billion active parameters and a 1 million token context window. Meituan says it trained and serves the model on domestic accelerators with under 80 gigabytes of memory per card, and it published a 4-bit build for them. Its API runs near 28 cents per million input tokens and $1.13 per million output tokens. Scores are self-measured, and the model trails Claude Opus 5 by about 15 points on Terminal Bench 4.0.

Confidence: Likely (model card via Silicon Noon; self-reported benchmarks)

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Texas has stopped switching on new large data center loads

ERCOT, the Texas grid operator, has paused approvals to energize new data center and crypto-mining loads of 75 megawatts or more until its Batch Zero eligibility audit is complete; the report is due to the Texas utility commission on December 10. Loads already connected are unaffected. With Norway's pause on Monday, two of the most data-center-friendly grids are now gating new load at the same time.

Confidence: Likely (ERCOT filings and Utility Dive; pause scope from secondary summaries)
