02|The Big Three

Voters turn on the data center buildout

What it is: Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor's race after calling for an immediate moratorium on new data centers. Wells Fargo says Texas holds 27 percent of planned US data center gigawatts; Nasdaq 100 futures fell as much as 2.2 percent overnight, and Texas-exposed Cipher Mining fell 17 percent Wednesday.

Why it matters: The limit on AI capacity is now partly political. Morgan Stanley notes a governor can pause projects and permits by executive order, so sites with power and permits in hand gain value while unbuilt Texas projects may slip or move to other states.

AMD's data center sales nearly double

What it is: AMD reported record third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, with data center revenue of $8.55 billion, up 97 percent, on EPYC server processors and new Instinct MI450 graphics processing units (GPUs). AMD guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion and told analysts server revenue should double in 2027.

Why it matters: Chip demand is still compounding, and a second supplier is winning real share in AI racks next to NVIDIA. Election results threatened to overshadow the beat in overnight trading, so investors now discount demand by whether the data centers get built.

Washington closes the offshore rental door

What it is: Oracle cancelled a five-year, roughly $7 billion lease that gave Tencent access to about 100,000 advanced AI chips in Southeast Asia after US officials raised concerns, the Financial Times (FT) reported. Separately, Bloomberg reported Chinese firms ordered about 6 million NVIDIA RTX Pro 5500 chips worth about $70 billion but expect about 1 percent within four months.

Why it matters: Renting abroad was Chinese labs' main way around export limits, and pressure, not a new law, just ended one of the largest deals. Coming a day after NVIDIA's reported 49 percent cap, the message to every cloud is that Chinese demand is now a liability.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

Revolut cut AI coding costs about 90 percent with a self-hosted open model

Who and what: Revolut, the European digital bank, made Z.ai's open-weight GLM model, now GLM-5.3, the default coding model for about 2,000 engineers, served on its own fleet of about 56 NVIDIA B200 GPUs, with Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini kept as exceptions and image tasks routed to other models. David Abitbol, who leads its AI operations, presented the results at the AI Engineer conference in New York, reported by The Register on November 3.

Measured result: A blended cost of about $0.30 per million tokens, roughly a tenth of its Anthropic and OpenAI bill, plus a reported 60 percent improvement in coding time and 70 percent in lead time since self-hosting began around April 2026. Tag: Likely (company-reported; one slide shows 60 percent for lead time, and the trend does not isolate the model's effect).

Strength and weakness: Strength: flat, predictable billing meant engineers stopped rationing tokens and used AI for debugging and ticket work, where most of the time savings came from. Weakness: the model is text only, so vision work still goes to frontier models, and the weights alone need about 380 gigabytes of GPU memory.

Cost and time to deploy: Hardware cost not disclosed; about 56 B200 GPUs for 2,000 engineers. Self-hosting began around April 2026, about seven months ago.

Maturity: At scale.

Angle Health's voice agents resolve 90 percent of provider calls without a human

Who and what: Angle Health, which administers health coverage plans for small and midsize businesses, uses voice agents built on ElevenLabs' ElevenAgents platform to answer calls from medical practices about eligibility, benefits, claims, and prior authorizations in eight languages. The agents connect to its core administration system under Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) safeguards with zero data retention. Model: not disclosed. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: During the pilot the agents handled about 80 percent of provider call volume, resolved more than 90 percent of those calls end to end without transfer, and saved the team nearly 9,000 minutes a day. Tag: Likely (company and vendor reported; no outside audit).

Strength and weakness: Strength: routine lookups are answered at once around the clock, and the agents triage the calls they cannot handle to the right team. Weakness: still a pilot on narrow workflows, and every number comes from the buyer and the vendor.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Pilot.

4. Labs talk about limits and release a tool to prove what they serve.

OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman posted that people should not cede control of the future to superintelligence and that this requirement should ultimately constrain AI progress. Hours earlier, Anthropic open-sourced FlashProve, a research prototype that produces zero-knowledge proofs so a verifier can check on an ordinary processor that a response came from a committed model, without seeing its weights. Anthropic says the goal includes letting safety evaluators confirm that the model they test is the one served to customers. The prototype supports mixture-of-experts models, greedy decoding, and contexts up to 1,024 tokens, and is not maintained.

+Bull

Outside evaluators get a practical way to check that the tested model and the served model match, which makes outside oversight real.

−Bear

A post is not a policy, and FlashProve is an unmaintained prototype limited to short contexts.

My call (Certain on the post and the release, Guessing on the effect)
This is talk, not Tripwire 2. No lab capped its pace, but verifiable inference strengthens the checking half of Part 1.
Who wins
Auditors, regulators, and enterprise buyers.
Who loses
Providers that quietly swap cheaper models behind an endpoint (Guessing).

5. Google commits $15 billion to build in Malaysia.

Google said it will invest $15 billion in Malaysia in 2027 and 2028 to expand data center and cloud capacity, with new campuses in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, more than 1 gigawatt of clean energy added to the national grid, and water replenishment programs. The announcement came the morning after the US election.

+Bull

Hyperscalers can route around US permitting fights by building where power and approvals are available.

−Bear

Southeast Asia is also where Chinese firms rent US chips, and Washington just showed it will pressure deals there.

My call (Certain on the commitment, Guessing on any link to the election)
Offshore capacity for US companies is welcome; offshore capacity for Chinese renters is not. Southeast Asia keeps growing with stricter customer lists.
Who wins
Malaysian power, construction, and land suppliers.
Who loses
US states competing for the same hyperscale budgets (Guessing).

06|Unknown knowns
Public facts the market is underweighting. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

China ordered about 100 times the NVIDIA chips it expects to get

Bloomberg reported that Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, and other Chinese firms ordered about 6 million NVIDIA RTX Pro 5500 chips, the model built to fit export rules, worth about $70 billion, but expect only tens of thousands, about 1 percent, over the next four months because NVIDIA is prioritizing other customers and newer products. The shortage story is usually told about US buyers; this unserved Chinese order is a second, hidden backlog and a push toward domestic chips.

Confidence: Likely (single outlet, unnamed sources)

The viral $12 billion Vultr deal is a $1.2 billion order from September

Posts relaying AMD's results said Vultr will deploy the first AMD Helios systems by HPE in a $12 billion deal. HPE's own September 30 release puts the order at $1.2 billion, for racks of 72 AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs with EPYC Venice processors in Vultr's US data centers, and HPE also supplies Vultr with NVIDIA GB300 racks. Retold deal sizes are inflating tenfold in a market already nervous about AI spending, so check every figure at the source.

Confidence: Certain on the HPE figure

A Texas governor can pause data centers without the legislature

Morgan Stanley wrote that a governor can pause projects by executive order, direct agencies to halt pending permits, and appoint utility commissioners whose terms come up in 2027, while ending the sales tax exemption or letting localities reject projects needs the legislature. Projects already under construction are less exposed. Governor Abbott had already frozen environmental permits for data centers in September, so any moratorium starts from an existing pause, not from open approvals.

Confidence: Likely (analyst note as reported by Blockspace)

07|Unknown unknowns
Surprises from outside the big players. Each tagged Certain, Likely, or Guessing.

Qualcomm stacked phone memory on a processor and beat HBM4 on efficiency

Qualcomm validated its first High Bandwidth Compute test stack, which places low-power phone memory (LPDDR5X) dies directly on top of an AI compute die. The two-layer sample measured 18 times the bandwidth of external LPDDR5X and more than 5 times the bandwidth per watt of HBM4, the newest high-bandwidth memory, by the company's account, and its AI250 data center part is due to sample in the first half of 2027. If the design holds up at volume, inference chips could need less of the scarcest memory in the market.

Confidence: Likely on the measurements (company-reported), Guessing on production impact

Read more on GrokSee it on X

Moonshot missed its own deadline for Kimi K3.1 Pro open weights

Moonshot promised open weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro by November 4, and Together AI posted that the weights were dropping, but an outside check of Moonshot's Hugging Face page this morning found no official repository, only a gated, empty third-party upload. The model is available through Kimi's apps and application programming interface with a one million token context window. Chinese open-weight releases are what keep pressure on closed-model prices, so a slip matters.

Confidence: Likely (one outside check, early in the day)

11|State of the Thesis
Thesis status: Revised (Nov 4, 2026)
The belief: labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work, demand for compute keeps compounding, and the bottleneck moves to power, memory, and who finances the chips. Part 3 is Revised to add permission to build and to rent: Texas voters chose a governor who wants a data center moratorium, and US pressure ended Oracle's $7 billion Tencent lease. Parts 1 and 2 are Unchanged, with AMD's data center revenue up 97 percent and Anthropic open-sourcing a tool for outside checks.

Unchanged means nothing tested it. Revised means new evidence reshaped one part. Rewritten means a falsifier actually happened.

1. Labs keep shipping while outside groups check their work
Frontier labs keep releasing new models while regulators and outside researchers check their work, rather than pausing on their own. Key evidence: Anthropic open-sourced FlashProve so evaluators can verify that a served model matches the one they tested, and Sam Altman posted that human control should ultimately constrain AI progress, without setting any cap. Supporting note: Recursive self-improvement watch: The Information reported Anthropic is telling IPO investors its 15-person chip team uses Claude to speed chip design, and OpenAI posted that ChatGPT and Codex help its hardware teams iterate faster (Likely on the claims, Guessing on significance).
Unchanged
2. Demand for compute keeps compounding
Buyers keep needing more computing power, and cheaper models so far add to demand instead of cutting it. Key evidence: AMD's data center revenue rose 97 percent to $8.55 billion with about $17 billion of total revenue guided next quarter; Chinese firms ordered about $70 billion of NVIDIA chips; Google committed $15 billion in Malaysia. Supporting note: Open-weight price pressure: Revolut moved 2,000 engineers to a self-hosted open model at about a tenth of its frontier bill, and engineers used more AI once tokens were cheap, not less (Likely).
Unchanged
3. The bottleneck moves to power, memory, and who finances the chips
Chips alone no longer set the limit. Electricity, memory supply, who will finance the hardware, and now who has permission to build and to rent decide who ships. Key evidence: Texas elected a governor who wants a data center moratorium in a state with 27 percent of planned US data center gigawatts; US pressure ended Oracle's $7 billion Tencent lease; Chinese buyers expect about 1 percent of their NVIDIA order. Supporting note: Revised on Nov 4 to add permission to build and to rent as a fourth constraint. The financing leg still binds: Crusoe's chief executive said a multi-year compute contract with a startup is like a loan. No tripwire moved.
