02|The Big Three

Anthropic sets IPO terms, and its chip suppliers buy $26 billion

What it is: On Tuesday, November 10, Anthropic launched the roadshow for its initial public offering: 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, about 801.4 million of them sold by the company, listing on Nasdaq as ANT. At the top of the range Anthropic nets up to $64.35 billion and would be worth up to $1.405 trillion fully diluted, up from $965 billion in May but short of the $2 trillion discussed a few months ago. The amended filing adds a $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price from Google and NVIDIA ($10 billion each), AMD ($2.5 billion), Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft ($1 billion each), and TSMC ($500 million). CNBC reported pricing on November 18, trading on November 19, about 20 percent of shares for retail buyers, and a book already covered across the range.

Why it matters: The companies that sell Anthropic its chips, memory, and cloud are now buying its stock, so supplier cash helps fund the compute bill those same suppliers collect. Tripwire 1 moves from a price question to an execution question, and a covered book says public money still wants frontier labs after the neocloud selloff.

NVIDIA gets sued while the rest of the chip sector rallies

What it is: A shareholder filed a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose five minutes after Monday's close, alleging NVIDIA and Jensen Huang misled investors about Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive United States authorities reportedly concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset, and about NVIDIA's dealings with Megaspeed. The class covers buyers from October 9, 2025, to November 8, 2026, and lead plaintiff motions are due January 8, 2027. NVIDIA closed down 4.27 percent at $178.67, its lowest close since April 7, erasing about $190 billion of market value, while the S&P 500 had its best day since April. AMD rose 13.26 percent on an Oppenheimer upgrade and its Google TPU host win, Broadcom 8.78 percent, and Micron 6.25 percent.

Why it matters: Investors split the leader from the sector in one session: AI hardware demand still gets bought, but NVIDIA now carries legal, governance, and export-policy risk at once. Gavin Baker added that roughly 600,000 H200 chips sit in Hong Kong because Beijing, not just Washington, is blocking imports (Reuters, Likely).

OpenAI builds a stop button as Anthropic ships its strongest model

What it is: On Monday OpenAI described Control Room, a central system already deployed across its research infrastructure that tracks every research inference workload, including agentic sampling used in training, and halts model output within 35 seconds of a decision to stop a job. Every agentic research job must map to an approved safety case, and the lag comes from an authorization cache OpenAI wants to drive to zero. The same day Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, one underlying model with different safeguards, priced 20 percent lower at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, with record scores such as 49.1 percent on FrontierCode 2.0. Anthropic said outside evaluators including the UK AI Security Institute and METR tested the model before release, and that a team of 1,000 Fable agents formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours.

Why it matters: Part 1 of the thesis in one day: labs keep shipping faster, cheaper models while building containment for their own agents and inviting outside testers. Cheaper top-tier tokens also feed Part 2, since lower prices have so far raised usage rather than cut compute demand.

03|Use Cases
What superintelligence is actually doing in the world, and how well it works.

Northern Trust's onboarding agent cuts data prep from 2.5 weeks to three days

Who and what: Northern Trust and Microsoft Frontier Company forward deployed engineers built a client onboarding agent for Northern Trust's Front Office Solutions platform, which serves foundations, endowments, pensions, and family offices. The agent converts years of client transaction history into Northern Trust's standard reporting format, with analysts reviewing every output. Announced November 9; built on Microsoft Azure with Microsoft Foundry, Excel, and SharePoint. Model and chips: not disclosed.

Measured result: Data preparation time fell 83 percent, from about 2.5 weeks to three days, measured on real production onboarding cases; a 96.5 percent F1 first-pass quality score (a measure combining precision and recall) on seven test cases built from anonymized client workbooks. Tag: Likely (company-reported, with the method footnoted).

Strength and weakness: Strength: measured on production cases, not a demo, in a regulated workflow with human review built in. Weakness: the quality score rests on only seven test cases, and analysts still resolve exceptions by hand.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

An AI nurse handoff tool cuts handoff time 60 percent across 180-plus facilities

Who and what: Egen, a Google Cloud Premier Partner, deployed a nurse handoff tool that uses large language models to write structured patient summaries for shift changes across more than 180 facilities, announced November 10 alongside Google Cloud's Gemini for healthcare. The health system is not named; the model, chips, and cloud for this deployment are not disclosed.

Measured result: Handoff time fell 60 percent, with reported improvements in nurse and patient satisfaction but no satisfaction figures. Tag: Guessing on the method (partner-reported, unnamed client, no baseline published).

Strength and weakness: Strength: a daily clinical routine at real scale, with nurses keeping the final say. Weakness: no named client, sample size, or error rate, so the 60 percent cannot be checked.

Cost and time to deploy: Not disclosed.

Maturity: At scale.

Mythos 5.2 roughly doubles bug finds in Firefox hardening

Who and what: The team hardening the Firefox browser has run successive Claude models all year to find security flaws; Anthropic's November 9 launch post quotes them on Claude Mythos 5.2, available only to vetted organizations through its Cyber Verification Program. Chips and cloud: not disclosed.

Measured result: Mythos 5.2 roughly doubled the vulnerability discovery metrics of Mythos 5.1 across scanning campaigns; separately, a security company found one in six confirmed bugs from Mythos 5.2 were missed by every other frontier model it tested. Tag: Likely (tester quotes; no raw counts).

Strength and weakness: Strength: each model round leaves fewer, subtler bugs to find. Weakness: Anthropic's own footnote says vulnerability identification on its evaluations is roughly on par with Opus 5.5, and access is gated.

Cost and time to deploy: Mythos 5.2 lists at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens; campaign cost and duration not disclosed.

Maturity: Production.

1. Anthropic sets IPO terms, and its chip suppliers buy $26 billion: bull, bear, and my call

+Bull

A covered book and $26 billion of strategic money fund a compute bill near $900 billion and keep multi-vendor buildouts on schedule.

−Bear

Suppliers buying their customer's stock is circular financing; if Anthropic trades poorly, the same companies lose on both the shares and the orders.

The listing is now an execution test, not a timing or price test.
Who wins
Anthropic; underwriters; suppliers locking in their largest customer.
Who loses
Late private buyers who paid for $2 trillion; neoclouds competing for the same investor dollars.

4. Lambda misses a lease deadline on a $34.9 billion Anthropic contract.

Anthropic's filing says either side may end its August 2026 Lambda agreement, about $34.9 billion of $41.3 billion in Lambda commitments for NVIDIA GPU capacity through 2034, if Lambda does not secure the facility lease in time. Wire reports on Tuesday said Lambda missed that deadline, no termination notice has been sent, and Anthropic does not currently intend to end the deal. Fees accrue only as capacity is delivered and accepted.

+Bull

Anthropic staying shows the capacity is wanted, and fees start only when it arrives.

−Bear

A neocloud that cannot lock a building cannot deliver GPUs, and the exit right now sits with the buyer.

My call (Certain on the filing language; Likely on the missed deadline (wire reports relayed on X))
The constraint is the building and the power, not the chips.
Who wins
Anthropic's negotiating leverage; landlords with ready power.
Who loses
Lambda; lenders to unleased GPU projects.

5. Norway pauses new data center grid reservations until 2027.

Statnett, Norway's grid operator, stopped new grid-capacity reservations for data centers on Monday, expected to lift no later than August 1, 2027, while it writes technical rules for large, fast-changing loads. Existing connections are unaffected. Statnett reports 800 megawatts of data center load connected, 4,200 megawatts reserved, 9,100 megawatts queued, and inquiries for 18,000 more.

+Bull

Clear rules on staying connected through disturbances and on ramp rates make large AI loads safer to connect later.

−Bear

One of Europe's cheapest clean-power markets just closed its data center queue for about nine months.

My call (Certain (Statnett release; Grid & Silicon))
Grid operators, not only governors, now gate AI capacity. Similar rules in other tight grids are likely to follow (Guessing).
Who wins
Sites already connected or reserved in Norway; Nordic rivals with open queues.
Who loses
New Norwegian projects; neoclouds pitching Nordic capacity for 2026 and 2027.

A startup says a non-public Anthropic model proved an 18-year-old quantum conjecture

Physical Superintelligence (PSI) published a Lean-checked proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, a key step in setting limits for quantum computing, produced by its research system running a non-public Anthropic model; Scott Aaronson blogged that it is proved. The proof is public, but the model and process cannot be reproduced, and Anthropic did not say which model it was.
